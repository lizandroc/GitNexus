"we don't do labs."

## 2. The one-paragraph story

Medicare's GLP-1 Bridge went live July 1, 2026 — eligible Part D members now get Wegovy,
Zepbound or Foundayo for a $50 monthly copay, through December 2027. That puts a large,
previously priced-out population onto GLP-1s, and every one of them needs baseline and
periodic monitoring. At the same time, the compounding advantage that carried this category
for three years is closing: the FDA resolved the shortages and in April 2026 proposed
pulling the GLP-1s from the 503B bulks list entirely. So volume is going up while drug
margin goes down — and the organizations that win are the ones that can monitor properly
and get paid for it. myLAB Box supplies that: a 10-biomarker at-home panel, CLIA-certified
labs, physician-reviewed results, no inventory, no minimums.

- **S1 Clinic / med spa:** "Your patients need labs every 3–4 months anyway. Recommend the kit, they order through the portal, you earn on every one — and you never touch inventory."

## 5. Discovery questions

**Opening**
1. Has the Medicare Bridge changed your GLP-1 volume yet, or are you still waiting to see it?
2. Walk me through what happens between a patient saying yes and their first dose.
3. Where do labs sit today — required, optional, or not part of the program?

**Qualifying**
4. Roughly how many kits a month would that be — under 25, 25–100, 100–500, or more?
5. Who owns the clinical protocol — a medical director, a PIC, a partner group?
6. Are you looking to earn on the test yourself, or to buy at volume and supply your population? *(routes Motion A vs B)*
7. What states do you operate in? *(NY check — P15)*

**Building the case**
8. What share of consults never reach a first dose? Do you measure it?
9. What does month-3 retention look like versus month-1?
10. If a patient needs a follow-up panel today, what actually happens?

**Closing**
11. Who else needs to be in the room — clinical, ops, legal, procurement?
12. What's the timeline, and what's driving it?

## 6. Objection handling

**"We already send patients to LabCorp/Quest."**
> That works clinically — the question is what it costs in conversion. Every draw-site trip
> is a step where a patient stalls. Same panel, no trip: collection is about five minutes at
> home, results back in 3–5 days from when the lab receives it. Worth measuring how many of
> your consults never complete that draw?

**"We don't require labs."**
> Plenty of programs don't. What's changing is volume — Bridge is putting a lot more people
> on these drugs, including older patients with more comorbidity. "We didn't check baseline
> thyroid, kidney and liver function" gets harder to defend as the population widens. Your
> medical director should set the protocol; what we do is make it operationally free to follow one.

**"What do we actually make on it?"**
> Pricing is volume-tiered, so I'd rather quote you properly than guess. What I can tell you
> is the structure: you recommend, the patient orders through the provider portal, you earn
> on every kit and every reorder — and because monitoring runs every 3–4 months, that's 3–4
> orders per patient per year, not one. Give me your rough monthly volume and I'll get you real numbers.

**"Too expensive / margins are already thin."**
> Thin margins are the reason to look. Drug margin is compressing for everyone as compounded
> supply closes. The panel is a program component you can earn on, not overhead you absorb —
> and there are no minimums, so it doesn't cost you anything to find out.

**"We'd need to build an integration."**
> For the provider program there's nothing to build — it runs through our portal. For
> enterprise volume, kits ship direct to your population and results come back digitally,
> with an account manager on the rollout. We handle kits, shipping, lab processing and results.

**"How do we know the results are any good?"**
> Fair question, and the honest answer is accreditation: CLIA-certified, CAP-accredited lab
> affiliates, HIPAA-compliant reporting, results comparable to a sample collected in a
> physician's office. It's not a rapid test — it's a real lab panel, physician-reviewed. And
> when a result needs attention, the patient gets a free consult with a physician licensed
> in their state.

**"Our legal/compliance team will never approve this."**
> Reasonable — and for the provider program there's a compensation structure your counsel
> will want to look at, so let's put that in front of them early rather than late. I can send
> the CLIA/CAP documentation and a draft BAA up front and let your team shred it before we
> spend your time on a demo.

**"We're building this in-house."**
> Some organizations should. The build is CLIA lab relationships, kit engineering,
> fulfillment, results infrastructure and state-by-state licensure. If the goal is to be
> ready for Bridge volume this year, white-label first and build later is usually cheaper.

**"Send me information."**
> Happy to — one question so I send the right thing: are you looking to earn on the test
> with your own patients, or to buy at volume for a population? They're different programs.

**"Not a priority right now."**
> Fair. Bridge runs through the end of 2027, so the volume question doesn't go away. When
> does it actually hit your P&L — this quarter or next? I'll come back a month before.

## 7. Tone rules

**Do:** lead with their change, not our product · ask more than you assert · short sentences,
mobile-legible · name the specific friction (the draw site) not the abstract benefit · always
offer an easy out · disclose the NY limitation before they find it.

**Don't:** open with "I hope this finds you well" · use "revolutionary," "game-changing,"
"synergy" · fabricate a referral or a prior conversation · quote or estimate partner earnings ·
send more than one message per channel per week · claim a customer result we can't cite ·
advise on their clinical protocol or regulatory posture.
